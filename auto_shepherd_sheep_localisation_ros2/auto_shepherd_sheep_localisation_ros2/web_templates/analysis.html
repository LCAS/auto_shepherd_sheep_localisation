The three parameters

The fitted gains are retained for research. The 0–1 indicators below are easier-to-read summaries calculated using fixed interaction distances; they are not percentages or health scores.

ParameterWhat it measuresLow value (0)High value (1)
CohesionHow strongly a sheep stays with or moves towards its local groupIsolated or moving awayStaying close or moving towards the flock
AlignmentHow closely a sheep matches its neighbours’ movement directionMoving in a different directionMoving in the same direction
SeparationHow strongly a sheep avoids getting too close to othersLittle avoidance responseStrong avoidance response

How to interpret the scales

Cohesion: high values indicate movement towards or remaining near the local group. Low values can indicate drifting away or isolation.

Alignment: high values mean similar travel directions. Low values indicate a different direction; very slow sheep are treated as having no reliable heading.

Separation: high values indicate a close neighbour and a stronger avoidance influence. Low values usually mean neighbours are sufficiently far away.

How inference size and tiling work

Choose the mode and size from the Replay controls. Larger inputs can help detect small sheep, but increase processing time and GPU memory use.

SettingWhat happensBest useTrade-off
Full frame · 640The complete original frame is resized internally to a 640px inference canvas.Fast baselineSmall sheep may be harder to detect.
Full frame · 960 / 1280The complete original frame is resized internally to a larger canvas.Test improved small-sheep detailSlower and uses more GPU memory.
2×2 tiledThe original frame is split into four quadrants. Each becomes a 640×640 tile, then YOLO runs on the combined 1280×1280 image.Experimental small-object testHighest memory use; IDs may change at tile boundaries.

Important: tiled mode does not first shrink the whole frame to 640. It splits the original frame first, and currently uses a fixed 1280 inference canvas.

How movement screening works

A screening candidate is an observation whose recent movement differs substantially from the other currently visible tracks. These are exploratory relative thresholds for prioritising inspection, not medical, welfare, or lameness thresholds.

Screening ruleCurrent settingWhat it means
Comparison window60 secondsSpeed and stopping statistics use each observation's recent 60 seconds of usable history.
Minimum track history10 usable speed samplesAn observation is not compared until enough movement samples are available.
Minimum flock size3 eligible observationsThe screen waits for at least three comparable tracks in the current flock.
Unusualness threshold3.5 robust z-scoreOne or more measures must be unusually different from the observed flock: low speed, frequent stopping, or distance from the flock centre.
Stopped speed≤ 0.2 m/sSpeeds at or below this value contribute to the stopping fraction.
Persistence5 secondsThe unusual movement must continue before it appears as a current candidate.
Tracking gap tolerance2 secondsA longer gap resets the persistence timer rather than assuming continuous behaviour.

Important: the 3.5 threshold is relative to the observed flock, not a fixed speed or distance in metres. Orange video boxes mark current candidates; grey entries in the list are previous candidates. A candidate is not a diagnosis and should be checked against the video and the animal in person.
